NATIVE PI0 ON LIBERO_90: FAILURE MODE SUMMARY (ALL 90 TASKS)
=============================================================
Written 2026-10-08. Replaces the earlier 19-rollout version.
Source: Documentation/native_pi0_libero90/ (90 rollouts, one per task, init
state 0; 12 successes, 78 failures; 224x224 agentview video at 10 fps).
Checkpoint: pi0_libero, seed 7, replan_steps=5, max_steps=400, i.e. the loop
of openpi's examples/libero/main.py (collected with
failure_agent_prototype/tools/collect_native.py; results.json in the same
folder lists success and steps per task).
Per-task labels and descriptions: native_pi0_libero90_survey_labels.md.

Context: libero_90 was NOT in pi0_libero's fine-tuning data (libero_10, goal,
object and spatial were), so every rollout here is pi0 on a task it has not
seen. libero_10 does share 9 of libero_90's 20 scenes (section 3).

Method: every failed rollout was viewed as a 24-frame timestamped contact
sheet, with enlarged frames where needed, by six parallel reviewers (13 tasks
each), against the taxonomy below; reviewers could propose new labels.
Per-episode pixel motion: tools/video_motion_stats.py (appendix A).


---------------------------------------------------
1. HEADLINE FINDINGS
---------------------------------------------------

- Native success 12/90 (13%): tasks 10, 19, 24, 33, 38, 47, 57, 60, 67, 68,
  70, 79. All 5 successes of the earlier 19-rollout sample (24, 60, 68, 70, 79)
  succeeded again. Successful episodes take 10-36 s.
- Every failure runs to the 400-step cap. pi0 never stops on its own.
- The dominant failure is not motor skill but WHAT pi0 does: in 56 of 78
  failures the primary label is wrong object (R1, 32) or scene habit (R2, 24).
  pi0 typically runs a clean, confident version of a task it was trained on
  in a similar scene (the libero_10 task of the same scene, "close the drawer",
  "put the bowl on the plate", "put the cream cheese in the basket"), whatever
  the instruction says.
- Grasps are usually clean. Reviewers listed many clean pick-and-place
  executions on the wrong object or into the wrong place. The binding of
  instruction -> target and instruction -> destination is what fails.
- Stalling in the old sense (motionless) is rare: median still% of failures
  is 1%, only 6/78 have a still stretch >= 5 s. Most failures keep moving:
  dithering, adjusting without releasing, hovering over an empty spot. A
  frame-difference stall detector misses these.


---------------------------------------------------
2. FAILURE TAXONOMY (v2)
---------------------------------------------------

Two layers: ROOT CAUSES (decided after the fact) and SYMPTOMS (detectable
online). Counts are over the 78 failed rollouts: primary label / any label
(primary or secondary). R5-R7 and S4 are new; they merge the labels the
reviewers proposed (see the companion file).

ROOT CAUSES

R1  Wrong object (motor execution fine, target wrong)               32 / 39
    pi0 grasps a salient or habitual object: cream cheese and cans in the
    basket/tray scenes (46, 49, 50, 51, 55, 56, 58), the white bowl instead of
    the frying pan in KITCHEN_SCENE9 (40, 41, 42; 4/4 runs), the red/white
    mug instead of the book (80, 83), the wrong bowl of three (12, 13, 15),
    the wrong drawer (6, 11: "top"/"bottom" ignored). Often serial: after one
    wrong pick-and-place it picks a second non-target object (46, 49, 50, 58).

R2  Scene habit (runs a training task of a similar scene)            24 / 42
    - KITCHEN_SCENE10 (0-5): pushes the top drawer shut whatever is asked.
    - KITCHEN_SCENE4 (22, 23, 25, 26): bowl into the bottom drawer (libero_10).
    - KITCHEN_SCENE5 (28, 30, 31): ketchup into / closing the top drawer.
    - LIVING_ROOM_SCENE5/6 (65, 66, 69, 71, 72): the libero_10 two-mug or
      mug + pudding routine.
    - STUDY_SCENE1 (73-75): book into the BACK compartment (libero_10).
    - KITCHEN_SCENE6 (34): yellow-white mug carried to the microwave (libero_10).
    - Sub-steps out of order (4, 8): closes the drawer before the butter is in;
      carries the bowl to a closed drawer.

R3  Stopped short on an articulation                                  5 / 8
    Drawer pushed most of the way (0, 5), stove knob missed or brushed but not
    turned (20, 44, 45), microwave door swung ~15 deg then released (35).

R4  Placement                                                         4 / 12
    Right object at the destination but wrong compartment (78, 73-75 as
    secondary), tipped while adjusting without releasing (74, 75, 55),
    left on the plate rim (72), knocked the drawer shut while lowering (29).

R5  Target never reached / never grasped (NEW)                        7 / 13
    The goal object or fixture is never engaged:
    - phantom target: hovers over an EMPTY spot where the object sits in a
      sibling scene (81, 82: left of the caddy, as in STUDY_SCENE1/2);
    - target in an unusual raised spot, never approached (36, 37: bowl on top
      of the microwave);
    - dithering between candidates, never closing the gripper (32, 53);
    - grasp failure: reaches it but knocks it over and cannot re-grasp (27).

R6  Destination missed (NEW)                                          3 / 11
    The right object is grasped but taken somewhere unrelated to the goal
    receptacle or dropped in transit: all four STUDY_SCENE4 shelf tasks run
    the same "grasp the image-left book, carry it away from the shelf"
    routine (86-89); bowl slips out of the gripper mid-carry (43).

R7  Partial compound task (NEW)                                       3 / 5
    Two-step instruction: one sub-goal done cleanly, the next never started
    (63, 64: bottom bowl into the tray, never stacked; 1: drawer closed, bowl
    never moved, then the drawer re-opened).

SYMPTOMS (detectable online)

S1  Stall / dithering without progress
    Motionless stalls are rare (still% >= 20 in 11/78); much more common is
    continuous small motion with no contact progress. Detect from the absence
    of grasp / release / predicate / articulation events, not frame difference.

S2  Knocking objects over / pushing them in clutter
    Cartons and bottles in LIVING_ROOM_SCENE2 (51, 52, 53), the moka pot (18,
    21, 39), mugs (69, 71), the plate flipped (14, 16).

S3  Arm leaves the camera view
    Holding a book (86, 87, 89, 83) or after missing the stove knob (39, 44, 45).

S4  Wrist wind-up (NEW)
    The wrist rolls into an inverted, contorted pose never seen in the demos;
    afterwards the actions stop being purposeful (35, 36, 39, 43, 81).

Other observations (not separate labels): regrasp fumbling that eats the
time budget (22, 25); the correct action under way when the 400-step cap
hits after a wrong-object detour (56, also 52, 54).


---------------------------------------------------
3. TRAINING-SCENE OVERLAP
---------------------------------------------------

libero_10 (in pi0_libero's training data) uses 9 libero_90 scenes:
KITCHEN_SCENE3, 4, 6, 8, LIVING_ROOM_SCENE1, 2, 5, 6, STUDY_SCENE1.

                              tasks   native success   primary R2 among failures
  shared with libero_10         35      8 (23%)            13 / 27
  other scenes                  55      4 ( 7%)            11 / 51

pi0 does better where the scene is familiar, and where it fails there, it
most often runs the familiar libero_10 task instead. In other scenes it fails
more by grabbing the wrong object (R1 22/51) or never engaging the target.


---------------------------------------------------
4. MOTION-PRIMITIVE CANDIDATES (clean, repeatable executions)
---------------------------------------------------

- Push an open drawer shut: KITCHEN_SCENE10 tasks 0-4 (~9-15 s each), 30, 31.
- Bowl into the open bottom drawer, then push it shut (23, 26; bowl in by
  ~18 s, drawer shut by ~25 s).
- Top-down grasp of a bowl and carry (8, 9, 15, 17, 40-43; ~5-10 s).
- Object into a basket or tray: cream cheese (most repeatable; 46, 49, 50,
  51, 55, 56), cans (49, 46, 58), butter (50), cartons (52, 54).
- Mug onto a plate (65, 66, 69, 71, 72); the libero_10 two-mug routine (65).
- Book into the back caddy compartment (73-75, 76, 78).
- Turn the stove knob on (18, 21; ~10 s), when the scene is KITCHEN_SCENE3.
Not reliable: the stove knob in KITCHEN_SCENE9 (44, 45), grasping in the
tight carton cluster (51-53), grasping the frying pan (never once).
pi0 also never lets go and backs away after placing in a caddy (76, 78, 80,
85), so a scripted release-and-retract is worth having.


---------------------------------------------------
5. DISTILLING THIS INTO A SKILL LIBRARY
---------------------------------------------------

1. Split WHERE from HOW. pi0's grasps are good and its target/destination
   binding is bad on unseen tasks. So the coordinator should supply the
   target location (move the open gripper over the right object) and the
   destination (carry the grasped object there), and leave the grasp itself
   to pi0. This is the main design change in failure_agent_prototype v1.
2. Keep the fixed vocabulary (R1-R7, S1-S4) so the coordinator can look up
   entries reliably; "other" remains the way to propose additions.
3. Label with simulator state, not only video (see section 7 of the previous
   version: video at 224 px misreads which object is held in clutter).
4. Detect "no progress" from task events, not pixel motion (section 1).
5. Decompose multi-step instructions into single steps and verify each BDDL
   predicate (R2 out-of-order, R7 partial).


---------------------------------------------------
6. CAVEATS
---------------------------------------------------

- One rollout per task, init 0; pi0 is stochastic, so a rerun of the same
  task and init can fail differently.
- Video only: object identity in clutter and the exact final placement are
  best readings (confidence is recorded per task in the companion file).
- Reviewers named the libero_10 task being imitated from the visual pattern;
  the scene overlap itself was checked against the suite definitions.

7. ADDENDUM (2026-10-08): SIM GROUND TRUTH AND RECOVERY RESULTS
---------------------------------------------------

The 78 failed tasks were re-run with failure_agent_prototype v1 at init
states 0-2, once natively (observe mode, sim-ground-truth diagnosis at the
end) and once with the agent (logs/survey90).

Native pi0 on these 234 episodes: 5 successes (2%). Sim diagnoses of the 229
native failures:
  R1 wrong object 76, R5 target not reached 56, R6 destination missed 31,
  R3 stopped short 22, R2 scene habit 21, R4 placement 15, R7 partial 8.
Every failure got a root cause; none fell back to a symptom-only label.
The sim label is one of the video-survey labels (primary or secondary) in
162/229 episodes, the primary label in 123/229 (survey labels come from a
single init-0 video, so other inits can genuinely fail differently).

Where sim and video disagree:
- R5 is far more common in sim (56) than as a video primary label (7). When
  pi0 runs a sibling-scene routine that never touches anything (hovering at
  the drawer, over an empty spot, at the caddy), video reviewers called it
  R2 scene habit or R1; in sim nothing was grasped and no joint moved, so it
  is "target never reached". The two are the same behaviour seen from
  different sides: the habit is the cause, the unengaged target is what can
  be detected.
- R2 is detectable in sim only when a non-goal joint moves or a sub-step
  happens out of order (21); habits that only move objects show up as R1.
- R6 (31) is more common than on video (3 primary): in the shelf scene and
  with bowls the right object is carried around without reaching the goal,
  which the reviewers often folded into R1/R2.

Agent results (details: failure_agent_prototype/README.md and
prototyping_log.md): 102/234 successes (44%; 77 within the native 400-step
budget), 42 of the 78 tasks solved at least once. On the 14 tasks of the
earlier survey: 22/42 (previous prototype: 8/42). Most recoveries succeed
through the "coordinator supplies where, pi0 supplies the grasp" recipe of
section 5: 85 of the 102 successes ended with a plan that included the
scripted carry to the destination, 11 with the scripted drawer push. Those
primitives read object and handle positions from the simulator; on hardware
a VLM coordinator would have to provide them.
